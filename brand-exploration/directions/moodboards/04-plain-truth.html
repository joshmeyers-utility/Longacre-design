Positioning
The site that answers the question you actually asked. Homer City Energy Campus does not persuade; it posts the receipt. Figures are the hero, set huge. The footnote, the qualifier and the "as of" date are part of the poster, not fine print under it.
Drawn from
Eager: "we told the truth" — giant condensed caps, the real fruit filling the frame, stacked colour-blocked cartons, a category's myths broken plainly. Wise: one bright owned colour, big bold type on outdoor, the product in the world.
Voice test
"Up to 4.4 GW. Expected. On this site." — no other energy company can say that sentence, and it keeps the source's own qualifiers.

Power generation · Question 6 of 6

As the campus grows, will it drive up my power bill?

Electricity rates are reviewed and approved by the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission, and we do not have a role in determining those rates for the community. However, the plant is designed to increase the availability of electricity for local homes and businesses, which relieves pressure on the grid. In addition, the plant will operate 40% more efficiently on a heat rate basis than the former plant.

Mobile is the primary comp. Figures run at 112px, footnotes at 16px — the same reading size as body copy. Each block is one colour, stacked like Eager's cartons, so a scroll reads as a pile of receipts.

Motion: blocks wipe up on scroll-into-view (Interactions 2.0, 400ms, ease-out, 24px). Figures never count up — a number that animates through wrong values on its way to the right one is a small lie.
