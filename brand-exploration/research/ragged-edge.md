## What their work does (seen in the frames)

1. **One saturated colour field, full-bleed and owned.** Palmetto
   red-orange, Wise lime, Tilt yellow, Eager orange, Granola chartreuse on
   near-black. The colour is the brand. No pastel, no polite tints.
2. **Big, heavy, confident type, mixed with serif italic.**
   - Extended or heavy grotesques (the agency's own wide caps; Eager's
     condensed caps; Palmetto's bold italic wordmark).
   - Interrupted by an expressive serif italic for the human word: Tilt's
     "Credit for what *you're* capable of", "*Value* is already in you",
     and Globe-Trotter's "128 years and *counting*".
3. **Headlines that talk like a person.** "Why wait?", "Dollars for what
   you're doing", "There's nothing average about our insurance". Short,
   plain, specific, a little cheeky.
4. **Real people as portraits.** Tilt's grid of named working people, each
   with a caption. Marshmallow's faces. Shot with warmth and styled colour,
   not stock.
5. **Real things, big.** Eager's oranges and apples fill the frame.
   Palmetto's van, the Wise card, Free Soul's tubs. The product in the
   world.
6. **The brand lives on physical things.** A van livery, billboards, metro
   posters, packaging, merch, shop fronts. Every case study shows the
   identity out in the world.
7. **Heritage reinvigorated, not nostalgic.** Globe-Trotter's crest and
   serif with modern air. Papier's fan mark and refined caps.
8. **A playful system.** Marshmallow's characters, Omlet's colour blocks,
   Granola's oversized "Of".
9. **The agency's own site:** an ultra-wide extended grotesque in caps; a
   full-bleed blurred, moving colour field; serif pill navigation; serif
   long copy; generous scale.

## Palmetto is the closest analogue

A US home-energy brand that "made clean energy irresistible". It moved from
a sector that is easy to tune out to warm red-orange, a bold italic
wordmark, plain talk ("Why wait?"), a van livery, real homes and an
illustrated house. It proves energy can look like this.

## How this meets the Homer City brief

- **Boldness serves transparency, not spin.** Headlines are plain and
  specific (Ragged Edge's voice already is), figures are set big, and the
  footnote and "as of" date go with them, set as part of the type rather
  than hidden in fine print.
- **Real people and real things, big.** That means the trades, the place,
  the build. The supplied imagery is suspected generated (E15), so mood
  boards use labelled stand-ins and briefed placeholders.
- **The brand in the world:** site hoarding on Power Plant Road, hard-hat
  stickers, hi-vis patches, the alert banner, social cards (with
  footnotes), a fact-sheet cover, the construction-update email.
- **Colour:** Anchored by default. The five client colours used as owned,
  full-bleed fields, not tints. The stretch direction may go Open and must
  state how it relates to the client's colours.
- **Watch-outs:**
  - Kiewit's yellow and black. Gold fields need navy or cream type, never
    black, and no squared caps.
  - An aqua field next to the water question.
  - Every figure still needs its footnote and "as of" date, at any size.
