# Research: architecture and construction

Angle: architecture, building-design and construction/EPC firm identities and
project sites. How they show work in progress, site and aerial photography,
timelines, before/after, and the people doing the work.

Method note. Pages were read through a text-to-markdown fetcher, so it saw
structure, copy, alt text and filenames, not rendered pixels. Type and colour
are therefore **not confirmed** for most references; where a row says "verify",
open the site and look before it goes on the research board. Facts quoted
below come from those pages or search results and are reference only, not
Homer City copy. No instructions aimed at an AI were found in any page read.

## References

### 1. Kiewit (context: the EPC on this project)
URL: https://www.kiewit.com
- Distinctive: hero slider, then alternating image and text blocks, a tabbed
  service-line section, a market grid and a statistics band ($18.2B revenue,
  34,500 employees, ENR rank). Photography is of large work, captioned with
  project names (Alamitos Energy Center, Three Rivers Energy Center); few
  portraits, workers appear in careers copy. Voice: short declarative
  fragments ("Build Your Best", "Engineering. Construction. Solutions.").
  Type and colour: verify.
- Why it matters: it is the partner's public face. The campus site must sit
  beside it without echoing it. Its people-light, project-heavy posture is the
  gap this brief can fill.
- Webflow: easy (tabs, grid, mega-menu are native).

### 2. Gateway Development Commission, Hudson Tunnel
URL: https://www.gatewayprogram.org
- Distinctive: civic megaproject hub. Headline "Delivering the Most Urgent
  Infrastructure Project in America", a stats block (jobs, economic activity),
  a project map, ten construction packages each with photo, purpose, job
  count and location, and a "See Our Progress" section of site cameras you can
  pan, zoom and step back through in time. Also a Public Information Center,
  fact sheets in English and Spanish, photo and video galleries. Some images
  are labelled as simulations. Voice: formal, urgent, national stakes.
- Why it matters: closest model for "information hub" plus progress evidence.
  Per-package cards that carry a job count and a place are a usable pattern;
  simulations are labelled as such, matching our render rule.
- Webflow: cameras are an embed or third-party feed (flag for build notes);
  package cards are a CMS collection, easy.

### 3. Thames Tideway Tunnel
URL: https://www.tideway.london
- Distinctive: nav items carry status labels ("Now open", "Site completed");
  a "What's happening near me?" location finder; a counter with a visible
  "Data last updated" line; large numbers translated into things a neighbour
  can picture (1 million cubic metres as 400 Olympic pools); headline figure
  paired with a footnote; "Meet the People Behind the Tunnel". Voice:
  friendly, first person plural, a little self-deprecating ("even if we do say
  so ourselves"), a 24-hour helpline in the footer. Type and colour: verify.
- Why it matters: it is the best found example of date-stamping a moving
  number and using comparisons, both already house rules here. The "near me"
  idea suits neighbours.
- Webflow: status chips and dated counters are CMS fields, easy; a location
  finder needs an embed or simple filtered list.

### 4. Sizewell C
URL: https://www.sizewellc.com
- Distinctive: repeating rhythm of small orange icon, heading, short copy,
  link; a numbered milestone timeline 2012 to 2025; icon rows of stats (17,000
  jobs at peak, 7,900 in Suffolk, 1,500 apprentices); community fund figures;
  environmental figures (250ha habitat, 19% biodiversity net gain). Mix of
  progress photos and CGI. Voice: upbeat, promotional, British.
- Why it matters: shows the failure mode as much as the model. It addresses
  environment concerns with numbers but headline stats are not individually
  sourced on the page, and it has no visible Q and A. A sceptic gets claims,
  not receipts. Useful as the thing to beat.
- Webflow: easy.

### 5. Hinkley Point C (EDF)
URL: https://www.edfenergy.com/energy/nuclear-new-build-projects/hinkley-point-c
- Distinctive: dated project-update video ("Project Update May 2026"), a
  "Latest pictures" gallery, portrait photos of named staff including an
  apprentice, a local apprentice's career path, a community magazine, school
  involvement (children named the tunnelling machine), a way to report
  nuisance such as fly-parking. Facts as specific figures with comparisons.
  Search results give 1,740 apprentices, almost 70% from the South West,
  GBP 5.4bn spent with 1,500 regional businesses (EDF release, May 2026).
- Why it matters: the closest analogue for workforce and local-economy proof
  on a contested energy build. Staff-written first-person news is a voice
  worth studying. Note the page itself gives no completion percentage or
  schedule, which a transparency site should not copy.
- Webflow: easy; the corporate wrapper is the part to avoid.

### 6. Georgia Power, Vogtle 3 and 4
URL: https://www.georgiapower.com/nuclear (not confirmed live; coverage via
https://www.power-eng.com/nuclear/work-on-nuclear-power-units-in-georgia-reaches-10-million-hours/)
- Distinctive: progress told in site-specific units rather than adjectives:
  work hours (10 million), modules delivered (1,485 of 1,485), workforce
  headcount by trade (about 400 craft workers, mostly pipefitters and
  electricians, added in one summer), percent complete by date, and an online
  photo gallery of site progress. Voice: press-release plain.
- Why it matters: the nearest US precedent for a power build with thousands
  of craft workers. Counting hours, modules and trades is the register this
  campus (nine trades, apprentices) can borrow. Its record also warns that
  counts shifted between releases (8,000 versus 9,000 on site), which is why
  every number needs an "as of".
- Webflow: easy; the gallery is a CMS collection.

### 7. Sundt
URL: https://www.sundt.com
- Distinctive: "SKILL. GRIT. PURPOSE." tagline; "Every Sundt employee is also
  an owner"; project cards with location and sector labels; imagery from field
  crews (night bridge pour, safety week, groundbreakings); a Skilled Craft
  careers path. Uppercase section labels. Colour and type: verify.
- Why it matters: shows craft workers as the brand rather than a footnote.
  The uppercase-label habit is common here and is exactly what the current
  design system rules out (sentence case), so a direction may want to say so.
- Webflow: easy.

### 8. Mortenson
URL: https://www.mortenson.com
- Distinctive: "Let's Redefine Possible" and "We're builders at heart"; alt
  text suggests dusk exterior shots, substations and solar arrays, an
  excavator at sunrise; transparent header over photography with a white
  logo; featured project cards (location line then title); dated news grid
  with category tags; one client testimonial named with organisation. Mostly
  uppercase navigation. Few people shown. Type and colour: verify.
- Why it matters: strongest example of a builder using infrastructure and
  energy photography with a golden-hour sheen. That is what to differ from;
  it reads as any contractor, so it fails the "swap the name" test.
- Webflow: easy; header over photograph needs a scrim to meet the root
  CLAUDE.md contrast rule.

### 9. Studio Gang
URL: https://studiogang.com
- Distinctive: single long column; a values line ("Actionable Idealism"); an
  "In Her Own Words" quote block; projects get an outcome headline rather than
  a name ("Encouraging Exploration at a Historic Museum") plus one to three
  sentences on purpose and impact; photographer credited on every image
  (Iwan Baan, Jason O'Rear and others); four office addresses in the footer.
- Why it matters: an architecture firm leading with what a building does for
  people. Outcome-first headlines and photo credits both map to our "verifiable
  specifics" and attribution rules.
- Webflow: easy; credits fit a caption field.

### 10. BIG (Bjarke Ingels Group)
URL: https://www.big.dk
- Distinctive: a pure index: large hero image, a small mark, project name,
  and place, nothing else; neutral catalogue voice; every image alt text names
  the studio. Mixes photographs and proposed renderings in one grid with no
  visible distinction in the text capture (verify visually).
- Why it matters: the opposite of Kiewit's copy-heavy approach, and a warning:
  renderings and photographs sit side by side. Our rule requires a
  Photograph or Rendering flag; BIG shows why it is needed.
- Webflow: easy; progressive image loading is native lazy-load.

## Patterns

1. **Proof in site units, not adjectives.** Work hours, modules, packages,
   trades, apprentices, local share (Vogtle, Gateway, Hinkley). Fits the
   fact-discipline rules directly.
2. **Status on the thing itself.** Nav and cards carry "Now open", "Site
   completed", "Data last updated" (Tideway), so a date is part of the
   component, not the caption.
3. **Big numbers translated into pictures** (pools, pyramids). Tideway does it
   with swimming pools; the Giza comparison in our own canonical figures is the
   same move.
4. **Cameras and dated galleries for progress** (Gateway cameras, Hinkley
   "Latest pictures", Vogtle gallery). Evidence the build is real.
5. **Uppercase labels and a transparent header over golden-hour photography**
   (Mortenson, Sundt, Kiewit). Near universal in the sector, which is why it
   cannot be the distinctive move.

## Gaps nobody owns

1. **Named tradespeople as the identity.** Even Sundt and Hinkley keep workers
   as supporting imagery or a single apprentice profile. Nobody makes a
   specific trade, a union, a person with real wage and hometown the
   organising device of the site. Needs the nine union names (open question
   #2) and real photographs.
2. **Receipts next to claims.** Sizewell C quotes figures without per-stat
   sources; Tideway comes closest. No site found puts source, date and a
   downloadable document beside every figure, which is the sceptic's test.
3. **Neighbour questions answered where they are asked, including contested
   ones.** No reference surfaces an appeal, a permit or a water source as a
   first-class page. Note the water answer still has no canonical client
   source (open question #14), so this gap cannot be filled with invented copy.
