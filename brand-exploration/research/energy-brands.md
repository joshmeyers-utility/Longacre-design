# Research: energy brands

Angle: utility, generation, grid and energy-tech identities. Researcher:
brand-researcher, 2026-11-06. Source quality varies: where a detail was not
confirmed on a primary page it is marked **(unverified)** or **(not
published)**. Web content was treated as reference only; no embedded
instructions were seen in any page fetched. Mobbin not used.

Webflow difficulty: Low = native Designer. Med = Interactions 2.0 or tricky
layout. High = needs GSAP/Lottie/canvas embed (flag in build notes).

## References

### 1. EDP (Pentagram)
- URL: https://pentagram.com/work/edp/story
- Type: custom lowercase wordmark whose letterforms echo a spiral; supporting
  face FT Base, a modernist, "accessible and direct" family.
- Colour: earthy, grounded naturals stepping up to brighter accents; replaced
  a red palette.
- Motion: the identity began as motion. The spiral is drawn by a dot traced on
  a rotating surface, so form comes from movement.
- Imagery: wide landscapes and seascapes where people are small against
  powerful nature.
- Voice: "Changing tomorrow now". Aspirational, unified, one voice.
- Why it matters: strongest example of motion as an ownable idea, not
  decoration. Also the clearest example of the landscape-dwarfs-people
  convention we may want to avoid (people small, place abstract).
- Webflow: Med. Wordmark and palette easy; generative spiral is High (Lottie
  or canvas).

### 2. Ørsted (Kontrapunkt, with in-house team)
- URL: https://kontrapunkt.com/work/a-global-energy-company-goes-green
- Type: bespoke typography (names and weights not published on the page).
- Colour/imagery/motion: page names sound and animation styles and a series of
  photographs but gives no art-direction detail **(not published)**.
- Distinctive: a full system in one flag - digital brand centre, signage,
  wayfinding, ship and vehicle livery, publications, iconography. "Consistent
  yet expressive."
- Why it matters: shows an energy brand extended onto physical site assets
  (livery, wayfinding), relevant to a campus with gates, hoardings and
  vehicles. A renamed company (from DONG Energy) built a new meaning for itself
  rather than inheriting one.
- Webflow: Low for the site; the brand-centre idea is a CMS collection.

### 3. OVO (Wolff Olins; typeface by NB Studio)
- URL: https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/17-july-21-july-2023/wolff-olins-ovo-identity/
  (also https://transformmagazine.net/articles/2023/wolff-olins-re-energises-ovo-design-system)
- Distinctive: built from two assets only - the square logo and an ownable
  green - then widened the greens into a range. New dynamic layouts and
  iconography; "more tools for warmth and interactivity".
- Type: bespoke typeface was NB Studio's foundational work, not Wolff Olins'.
- Why it matters: proves a restrained system (one shape, one colour family)
  can be ownable in a category that looks interchangeable. Fits our anchored
  palette brief: vary use, do not add colours.
- Webflow: Low. Caveat: the LBB write-up returned 403, so layout details are
  from Design Week/Transform summaries only.

### 4. Octopus Energy
- URL: https://www.octopus.energy/
- Layout: long modular scroll, postcode box first, four large product tiles,
  image-plus-headline blocks, dated first-name customer reviews.
- Voice: playful wordplay ("Get more bang for your beams", "cheaper, greener,
  dreamier"). Mascot (Constantine the octopus) carries personality.
- Facts: big bold figures ("£7,500 grant", "4.8 stars") with hedges ("up to",
  "could") but comparative claims shown without visible sources in the fetched
  text.
- Why it matters: the best-known example of an energy brand with a human
  voice, and a warning. Its hedging vocabulary matches our "projected / up to"
  rule; its unsourced comparatives are exactly what a sceptic distrusts. Playful
  register is wrong for a permit-appeal context. Colours and typefaces were not
  recoverable from the fetch.
- Webflow: Low-Med.

### 5. Commonwealth Fusion Systems (Upstatement)
- URL: https://upstatement.com/case-study/commonwealth-fusion-systems/
- Layout: clean base "inspired by patent drawings".
- Imagery: three kinds only - zoomed-out "big picture" visuals, illustrated
  diagrams, real photographs from CFS's labs.
- Motion: explainer animations.
- Voice: optimistic, transparent, "trusted authority"; offers media an
  educational resource of accurate vocabulary. Written for hires, press, NGOs
  and environmental groups at once.
- Why it matters: closest brief match. A technology widely misunderstood,
  with sceptical NGOs among the audience, answered by diagrams and plain
  explanation. The patent-drawing idea suits an information hub. Page did not
  name typefaces or colours (not published).
- Webflow: Low for diagrams (SVG); explainer animations Med-High.

### 6. Skellefteå Kraft (BOLD, 2015)
- URL: https://www.lovethework.com/work-awards/entries/skellefte-kraft-103859
- Distinctive: a municipally rooted utility; logotype combines S and K, S for
  stable foundation and K for energy (per search summary). Reported brand
  recognition up 63% after launch (company-reported, unverified).
- Why it matters: a place-named, locally owned utility whose identity is its
  initials and its town, the posture we want. The award page itself held no
  design detail; the full case study (/work/campaigns/skellefte-kraft-246074)
  was not read.
- Webflow: Low.

### 7. Vattenfall (2018 refresh)
- URL: https://energywatch.com/EnergyNews/Renewables/article10460108.ece
- Distinctive: retained the name; refresh followed research with 3,000+
  respondents in four countries who found the old identity insufficiently
  distinctive.
- Why it matters: evidence-led identity change, and a reminder the category
  default problem is real - respondents could not tell the brand apart. Visual
  detail not confirmed.
- Webflow: Low.

### 8. Electricity Maps
- URL: https://electricitymaps.com/company (methodology:
  https://app.electricitymaps.com/docs/guides/methodology)
- Distinctive: open-source map, public methodology, free historical CSV data
  under an open database licence. Visual system not described in anything
  retrieved; inspect directly before borrowing.
- Why it matters: the model for "the sceptic can check". Transparency is
  shown as downloadable data and published method, not as an adjective.
- Webflow: map is High (embed); methodology and download pages Low.

### 9. Ember (energy data think tank)
- URL: https://ember-energy.org/data/electricity-data-explorer/
- Distinctive: public data explorer refreshed on a stated cadence, CC BY 4.0,
  API with free keys.
- Why it matters: "updated on a schedule, open licence, source stated" is the
  pattern behind our "as of {month} {year}" rule. Visual system not retrieved.
- Webflow: explorer High (embed); date-stamped CMS stat fields Low.

### 10. Sizewell C Works Tracker and Hinkley Point C community pages (EDF)
- URL: https://sizewellcsupplychain.co.uk/news/revamped-works-tracker-launched-to-improve-information-access-for-local-communities/
- Distinctive: a community-relations-run tracker of current and upcoming works;
  Hinkley's blog states "transparency is at the heart of everything we do".
  A planning submission criticises Sizewell's transparency (one stakeholder's
  view, not a finding).
- Why it matters: the nearest peers - big energy builds with local publics -
  and a live example that a tracker is demanded by forums, and that the claim of
  transparency is judged against it. Visuals not assessed; these read as
  functional, not designed.
- Webflow: Med (filterable CMS list of works).

## Clichés to avoid (named precisely)

1. **The turbine-at-sunset hero.** Wind turbine, pylon or gas stack in golden
   hour, centred, with a one-line headline. Interchangeable between any two
   energy companies.
2. **Handshake and hard-hat stock.** Staged models in clean PPE, diverse by
   formula, smiling at a tablet. Undermines the "real people, real trades"
   brief.
3. **The leaf-and-lightning-bolt mark** and its cousins: swoosh, globe with
   arrow, abstract flame. Gas companies especially use green leaves to borrow
   a credential they cannot document.
4. **Blue-to-green gradients** and glowing-line "network" backgrounds, plus
   neon-on-navy "future grid" visuals. They signal tech-vendor, not neighbour.
5. **Language:** "powering the future", "energising communities", "a brighter
   tomorrow", "sustainable solutions", "partnering for progress". EDP's
   "Changing tomorrow now" is the same family; it works only because the whole
   system is behind it.
6. **People small in vast landscape** (EDP). Elegant, but it makes the place
   scenic and the workforce anonymous, the opposite of our emphasis.
7. **Unsourced comparatives** (Octopus's "86% vs public charging" with no
   visible source). A sceptic reads the missing source as the answer.
8. **Mascots and wordplay** in a context with a live permit appeal.

## Patterns

1. **One ownable idea, applied everywhere** (EDP spiral, OVO square plus green,
   Skellefteå's initials). The strongest identities are narrow, not broad.
2. **Motion or system generated from a real mechanism**, not decorative
   animation (EDP's dot on a rotating surface).
3. **Explain the technology with diagrams and real lab/site photography**
   (CFS: patent-drawing base, diagrams, labs). Trust comes from showing the
   machine.
4. **Transparency expressed as artefacts**: published methodology, open data
   licences, stated update cadence (Electricity Maps, Ember), works trackers
   (Sizewell C). Not as copy.
5. **Identity extends onto physical assets** (Ørsted livery, wayfinding;
   brand centre), which this campus has in gates, signs and vehicles.

## Gaps nobody owns

1. **A named, local workforce as the identity.** No energy brand found leads
   with real tradespeople, trades and place (nine unions, craft names, local
   share) as the visual and verbal core. Workers appear as stock or
   scale figures.
2. **Sceptic-first design.** CFS educates; Octopus persuades; Sizewell
   publishes trackers. None found puts the contested topics (permit appeal,
   water, noise) up front with source, date and a downloadable document as the
   primary pattern.
3. **A specific place, in a specific county, as a brand.** Reviewed brands are
   corporate or national (EDP, Ørsted, OVO). Skellefteå is nearest, but no
   US energy build with a named town and a construction-in-progress story
   was found with a designed identity at all.

## Limits of this research

Primary pages for Ørsted, CFS and Skellefteå Kraft did not give type or colour
detail; OVO's LBB page returned 403; the search engine found no Koto or Collins
energy work, and no 2024-25 US utility rebrands from the companies queried.
Mobbin not queried. Typefaces and hex values above are only those stated by the
sources.
