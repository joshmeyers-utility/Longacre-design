### Slide 10

- Power Plant & Fuel Supply (Cont.)
- Power in Front of the Meter. All power produced on the Homer City Energy Campus will flow directly to the grid as a Front of the Meter transaction, and our customer will only take a portion of that power.
- More than $10 billion in initial investment in the power plant. Power infrastructure and site readiness will exceed $10 billion, with data center development expected to add billions more.
- Built by experienced partners. Kiewit Power Constructors is leading engineering, procurement and construction, and GE Vernova is supplying the turbines and combined-cycle equipment.
- Powering Homes and America’s Digital Future
- The Homer City Energy Campus will have enough generating capacity to serve multiple on-site customers and to supply power to thousands of homes on the local grid.
- The Homer City Energy Campus will help relieve pressure on the Mid-Atlantic region, which PJM — the nation’s largest grid operator — predicts could face an energy capacity deficiency by the end of this decade.
- Pennsylvania Gas. Two Supply Paths.
- Reliable power begins with reliable fuel. Homer City Generation’s fuel plan is designed to connect the campus to two major interstate pipeline systems, providing more than one route for Pennsylvania-produced Marcellus natural gas to reach the plant.
- Multiple supply paths available. The plan can draw from both Texas Eastern Transmission and Eastern Gas Transmission and Storage, providing an alternate supply path if one source is constrained.
- Two underground connections. The site has two underground “lateral” lines that connect Homer City to the existing natural gas network serving the region:
- Main Line: A 20-mile pipeline from Delmont to Homer City
- Backup Line: A 6-mile line from Blairsville to Homer City
- A more efficient fuel source. The new natural gas power plant will tap into a more efficient and environmentally responsible fuel source with natural gas from the Marcellus Shale delivered safely and quietly through underground pipelines.

### Slide 11

- Power Plant & Fuel Supply (Cont.)
- How It Works
- The Power Plant at the heart of the Homer City Energy Campus is designed to generate more electricity than our customer needs.
- The power is generated “in front of the meter,” meaning it is all sold onto the public grid.
- Our customer will draw an agreed amount of power at an agreed price under a long-term contract known as a Power Purchase Agreement (PPA).
- Because the plant will produce more power than the customer consumes, the rest stays on the grid – creating a new supply of electricity for everyone in this region at a time when demand is growing fast.

### Slide 25

- Water Stewardship
- Download our Water Fact Sheet Here
- The Homer City Energy Campus uses the same water source that served the former coal plant for over 50 years — the Two Lick Reservoir, which is owned and operated by Homer City Generation. As a result of our plans to manage water usage campus-wide, total water use across the Homer City Energy Campus — including the power plant and the future data centers — will be in line with the historical usage levels of the former coal plant when it was operational.1
- Two Lick Reservoir will continue as the source of water. Two Lick Reservoir was constructed as part of the former coal plant and will continue to be owned and operated by Homer City Generation. Forecast water usage for the Energy Campus will align with historical withdrawal levels.
- We treat and reuse water onsite. Homer City Generation’s state-of-the-art water treatment system will treat onsite stormwater and water withdrawn from Two Lick Creek. The state-of-the-art cooling systems will recycle treated water throughout the Energy Campus to minimize the withdrawal rate from Two Lick Creek.
- We are building a modern water treatment facility. We are investing hundreds of millions of dollars to create a modern water treatment facility that will improve the efficiency of the water withdrawn. This represents an important environmental investment that will deliver meaningful benefits by improving water quality before it is returned to the watershed, supporting long-term ecosystem health.

- Investment That Stays Here
- Capital Investment: An initial capital investment projected to exceed $10 billion for power infrastructure and site readiness,1 making this one of the largest such investments in Pennsylvania’s history.
- Jobs: Creation of nearly 10,000 direct on-site construction-related jobs2 along with approximately 1,000 total direct and indirect permanent high-paying positions3 in technology, operations and energy infrastructure.
- Workforce Development & Training: A pipeline of family-sustaining careers for both experienced tradespeople and the next generation of apprentices who are gaining hands-on training on the Homer City Energy Campus.
- Tax and Other Revenue: The Homer City Energy Campus will boost local school budgets, townships and the county through property taxes, as well as through taxes paid by the thousands of people who work to build and operate the site.
- Business Community: The Homer City Energy Campus is already generating significant economic activity throughout Indiana County and the surrounding region, supporting local businesses, restaurants and lodging providers while also engaging local partners on-site.
- Local Power: All power generated will be placed on the local grid, and our customer will only utilize a portion. The surplus power will be more than sufficient to supply electricity to thousands of homes and businesses throughout the region.
- Being a Good Neighbor
- Traffic and safety. Traffic improvements have been developed at intersections across Center, Burrell, Black Lick and White townships — new signals, turn lanes and signage — to keep traffic moving safely around the site.
